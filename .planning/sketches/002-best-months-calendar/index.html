Notes

  • Strength: Discrete cells map cleanly to "what's this month?" — most readable for quick scanning.
  • Weakness: Cells are flat — no sense of how close two months are or which is slightly better.
  • Overlap: Needs a dedicated third row, which adds vertical weight in the narrow sidebar.
  • Fits: Works at any width. Sidebar version is compact; wide version is even more scannable.

B. Diverging Bars

Each month has two bars on a center axis — Paris bar grows up, London bar grows down. Where both peak, a dot marks the center axis. Reads as a pulse / tug-of-war between two cities.

Notes

  • Strength: Continuous bar heights show how close two months are. Vertically efficient — overlap doesn't need a third row.
  • Weakness: "Going down" might feel negative for City B (visual hierarchy treats up-as-good). Mitigated by labeling.
  • Overlap: Dot on the center axis where both bars hit max. Compact, doesn't add height.
  • Fits: Works narrow and wide. Wide reads almost like a heartbeat across the year.

C. Suitability Curves

Continuous filled curves — Paris in teal, London in ochre, both partially transparent. Where they overlap (both at peak), the colors mix into the overlap olive automatically. Feels more chart-like, less calendar-like.

Notes

  • Strength: Smooth curves feel like the existing Climato chart language. Overlap is communicated automatically by color compositing — no extra row, no extra marker.
  • Weakness: Less "calendar"-feeling. Users scanning for a specific month have to find it on a smooth curve rather than a discrete cell.
  • Overlap: The "both ideal" band emerges as a single olive-shaded area where the two filled curves overlap at maximum.
  • Fits: Best at wider widths. At sidebar width, individual month resolution is fine but feels more abstract.
